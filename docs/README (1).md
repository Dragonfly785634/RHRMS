# Ruby Hill Animal Rescue — the spreadsheet

This is the file the rescue actually runs on. Not a cleaned version, not an export, not a schema.
The file.

I got it from the director the way a team lead usually gets client data — by asking for it. This
repository is where it lives now, so there's exactly one copy with one address, and "the version I
have" stops being a thing anyone can say.

## Before you open it

It's a mess. That's not a warning, it's the assignment. Four people can edit it, the current version
dates from January, and nobody ever agreed on how any of it should be written down. Every
irregularity you find is a decision somebody made under time pressure with an animal in front of
them. Your job this month is to read it the way
an archaeologist reads a midden — the garbage is the evidence.

So: **don't fix it.** Don't normalize it, don't dedupe it, don't tidy the columns. The instant you
clean it up you've thrown away the thing you were supposed to notice. Take notes on what's wrong
instead. Those notes are worth more than the corrected file, and by week 4 you'll see why.

## Rules

**This copy is canonical.** If your spreadsheet and this one disagree, this one is right and yours
has drifted. Re-download rather than argue.

**Read-only.** Nothing you do here comes back to me. When you have your own project repository,
commit a copy of this file into it — that's your team taking custody of a client artifact, and your
history will show what you had and when you got it.

**The filename stays.** Yes, it's called `MASTER copy FINAL (2).xlsx`. Yes, it hurts. Leave it.
Somewhere in a real building right now there is a file named exactly this and a person whose job
depends on it, and part of what you're learning is that you don't get to choose the world you
inherit. You'll have opinions about naming conventions by December. Have them then.

## If something looks wrong

Ask. If it's a question about the data — what a column means, why two rows look the same — that's a
question for the director, and you'll get your next window with him at the top of class. If it's a
question about the file itself — it won't open, a tab is missing — that one's mine.

Learning to tell those two apart is most of the course.
